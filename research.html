Our Research

Most biological systems are nonlinear and change over time. We develop data-driven methods to identify them from measured signals — and apply those methods across the brain, cancer and metabolism.

Current Projects

Tracking time-varying cerebral autoregulation

Recursive multivariate models that follow how autoregulation shifts with physiological state.

Physiological noise correction for fMRI

Response functions that remove heart-rate and respiration effects from resting-state signals.

Optimal, low-toxicity cancer therapy

Control-based therapy schedules that balance efficacy against toxicity and drug resistance.

Closed-loop glucose regulation

Patient-specific models driving model-predictive control for insulin delivery.

Past Projects

EEG functional brain networks in epilepsy

Multi-scale analysis of how functional brain networks reorganise over time in patients with epilepsy.

Deep brain stimulation in Parkinson's disease

Predicting clinical outcomes of deep brain stimulation from pre-operative signal features.

Brainstem respiratory network modelling

Nonlinear models of the neural circuits that generate and regulate the respiratory rhythm.

Tumour growth in de novo carcinogenesis

Model-based characterisation of tumour-growth dynamics in an experimental mouse model.
